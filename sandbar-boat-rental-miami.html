The tide decides, not the calendar

A sandbar is only a sandbar at the right stage of tide. Too high and it's just open water; too low and you can't get near it. The difference between a great day and an average one is usually somebody checking that before you book, not after.

Send us the date you want and we'll tell you what the water is doing that day and what time to aim for. Sometimes that means moving your start by two hours, and it's worth it.

Which boat, and why it matters here

For a sandbar day specifically, the Sea Ray Amberjack 32 is often the right answer — it's open, it's quick to get there, and for four or five people it costs less than putting the big boat on the same patch of water.

The Chris Craft 45 earns its keep on a longer sandbar day, because a bathroom and shade stop being a luxury somewhere around hour four.

What's already on board

The floating pool goes in as soon as we're anchored — it's the thing that turns a stop into an afternoon. Cooler on ice, drinking water, speaker and life jackets in every size are all standard, on all three boats.

Weekends the popular banks fill up and it becomes a social thing, boats rafted three deep and music everywhere. Midweek you can have one almost to yourselves. Both are good days; they're just not the same day, so tell us which one you're after.

What everybody asks

Sandbar day questions

Which sandbar do you go to?

It depends on the tide, the wind and the day. Biscayne Bay has several and they are not equally good at the same moment. The captain picks on the day — that judgement is most of what you're paying for.

Is the water shallow enough for kids?

At the right tide it's roughly waist to chest deep on an adult, over firm sand. We anchor so that non-swimmers can stand, and we carry child-size life jackets as standard.

Do you provide floats?

Yes. The floating pool mat goes in the water as soon as we anchor, and it's included on all three boats. You're welcome to bring your own inflatables as well.

Can we meet friends on another boat there?

Yes, and it's common. Tell us in advance and we'll raft alongside them so it's one group rather than two boats shouting across the water.
