About Book
Who you're booking

We're three boats and the people who run them

There's no office, no reservations centre, and no catalogue of boats that aren't ours. There are three boats in the water and a phone number the captain answers.

How this actually works in Miami

Most boat rental sites in Miami belong to brokers. They don't own anything: they take your enquiry, find somebody else's available boat, add their margin, and pass it along. That's why prices move, why nobody can tell you exactly what's on the boat, and why the captain who meets you at the dock is never the person who answered your messages.

We have three boats: a Chris Craft 45, a Sea Ray Sundancer 40 and a Sea Ray Amberjack 32. We maintain them ourselves. When you message us, you're messaging whoever will be at the wheel.

That doesn't make us bigger than anyone. It makes us easier to trust.

Four things we don't move on

These aren't marketing promises. They're the four rules people come back for.

The price is said first

Boat, captain and fuel in one figure, before you book. No booking fees and nothing that shows up at the dock.

The day happens in your language

Spanish or English, safety brief included. Half our groups arrive with someone who doesn't speak English, and that person has to understand exactly what everyone else understands.

The boat is ready before you are

We get there early: ice in the cooler, speaker charged, life jackets counted, and whatever you sent ahead already set up. You don't build a party with the guests watching.

What we post actually happened

Every photo and every clip on this site came off a real charter on one of our three boats. We don't buy catalogue photography.

Where the day goes

Fifteen minutes in and you're inside Biscayne Bay, which is where everything people came to see actually is: the sandbar, the islands, Star Island, the big houses and the downtown line.

The route isn't fixed. Tell us whether you want to swim, eat properly, take photos or just stay anchored all afternoon, and we build the day around that. The captain knows the hours of each spot: when the sandbar is crowded, when the light lands right on the city, when it's worth heading back.
