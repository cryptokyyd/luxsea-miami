The difference between a photo and the photo is the hour

Star Island, the waterfront houses and the downtown line — on a route shaped around where the light actually falls.

Book a photo run Send the details

Light on the city, not behind it

The downtown skyline faces roughly west across the bay, which means for most of the day you are shooting into your own light. Late afternoon is when the sun swings round and lands on the buildings instead of behind them, and that window is short.

We plan the route backwards from it. Star Island and the waterfront houses first, when the sun is still high and side-on, then the open bay for the skyline as the light drops.

Bring a photographer, or don't

Plenty of these charters go out with a hired photographer or a content team, and the boat gets treated as a set — we'll hold position, cut the engine, reposition for the angle, and do it again if the first pass didn't work.

Just as many go out with nobody but the group and a phone. Both are normal. The only thing that changes is how long we sit still, so tell us which when you book.

What actually photographs well from the water

Star Island and the houses along it, the cruise terminals when a ship is in, the bridges, and the downtown wall of towers from open water. On a clear evening the city lights come up while there's still colour in the sky, and that fifteen minutes is the best of it.

What doesn't work is the middle of the day in flat overhead sun with the skyline in haze. We'll say so when you ask for that slot rather than take the booking and let you find out.

What everybody asks

Skyline and photo questions

What's the best time of day for skyline photos?

Late afternoon into sunset. The downtown towers face west across the bay, so earlier in the day you're shooting into the light. The last hour before sunset, and the fifteen minutes after it when the lights come on, are the two best windows.

Can we bring a photographer or a drone?

A photographer, yes — it's common, and the captain will hold position and reposition for angles. Drones are a different matter: parts of Biscayne Bay sit under restricted airspace near the airport and the port, so ask before you plan around one.

Do you go past Star Island?

Yes, it's part of the standard route. We run past the island and the waterfront houses, then out to open water for the skyline. You can't go ashore anywhere private, but the whole run is photographable from the boat.

Is this good for a proposal?

It's the most common one we do after birthdays. Say so quietly when you book and we'll cut the engine at the moment you want, keep out of the frame, and take the photos afterwards if you'd like them.

Tell us what you're shooting

A content shoot, a family portrait and a proposal need three different routes. Say which and we'll build the day around it.
